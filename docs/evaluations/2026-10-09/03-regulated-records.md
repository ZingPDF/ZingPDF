# Evaluation 3: multinational regulated records and signature platform

Evaluation date: 9 October 2026. Role: technical evaluator for a hypothetical proprietary software company with 25 developers, US$100 million annual revenue, enterprise SaaS and customer-hosted Linux deployments. Company size and volumes below are scenario assumptions, not ZingPDF customer facts.

**Decision: select commercially licensed iText Core, pdfCalligraph and pdfSweep for the first controlled pilot. Do not approve production procurement yet.** ZingPDF fails several mandatory requirements in its current published contract. Aspose.PDF and Apryse Server SDK remain challenger candidates if iText fails the corpus, accessibility, deployment or commercial gates. This decision is about evidence of relevant APIs and risk reduction; no comparative implementation or production-quality benchmark was run.

## Business workflow and precise requirements

The business supplies regulated records software to financial-services and public-sector customers. It creates multilingual notices and consent records, receives externally signed documents, verifies signatures, prepares disclosure copies and retains evidence for ten years. It processes an assumed one million documents per year; normal inputs have 1–100 pages, with exceptional 2,000-page, 250 MB case bundles.

The mandatory scope has three separate artifact classes:

1. **Received evidence:** retain the exact original bytes and digest. Validate each signature and signed revision; report later unsigned changes, certification permissions, trust and revocation uncertainty separately. Never normalize, redact or convert the original while claiming its previous signatures still protect the transformed content.
2. **New controlled records:** create documents from structured business data and reviewed templates. Require PDF/A-2a archival output and PDF/UA-1 accessible output, preferably together on the same artifact. Embed licensed fonts, preserve Unicode mappings, supply document language, semantic tags, reading order, table headers and authored alternative text. Validate both declared profiles after signing. If simultaneous conformance cannot be demonstrated, the pilot fails unless the business explicitly approves separate linked archival and accessible deliverables.
3. **Disclosure derivatives:** produce a separately identified redacted copy of a received record. Remove selected text, image pixels and intersecting graphics; sanitize hidden text, metadata, annotations, attachments and previous revisions according to a documented policy. Preserve the original as access-controlled evidence and record a source digest and transformation audit outside the disclosed PDF. A derivative can receive a new signature; it does not inherit the original signature's protection.

| Gate | Mandatory acceptance scope | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| R1: existing PDFs | Handle normal PDF 1.4–2.0 files, object streams, incremental revisions, rotated pages, embedded/subset fonts, nested forms and encrypted input when credentials are supplied. Detect unsupported/damaged files and route them for review. | Customer documents are not generated by our own template engine. |
| R2: typography | English, French, German, Japanese, Arabic, Hebrew and Hindi; mixed RTL/LTR identifiers, combining marks, ligatures and line wrapping; searchable Unicode text. Fonts packaged locally. | A visually plausible Arabic string can still be misordered or incorrectly shaped. |
| R3: archive/accessibility | Controlled generation meeting the specified PDF/A and PDF/UA profiles, checked independently, with human accessibility review. Arbitrary untagged uploads may need specialist remediation. | An API flag or automatically added tag tree cannot supply missing document meaning. |
| R4: signing | PAdES Baseline B-T and B-LT creation, B-LTA upgrade and timestamp renewal; external/HSM signing interface; suitable algorithms and certificate-chain material; no exportable production private key requirement. | Signature longevity depends on more than embedding a detached CMS signature. |
| R5: validation | Byte integrity, signed revision coverage, trusted timestamp, issuer chains, embedded OCSP/CRL, DSS evidence and DocMDP changes assessed separately. Invalid, unknown and unsupported results must never become blanket valid outcomes. | Cryptographic verification alone does not establish trust or allowed modification. |
| R6: redaction | Rewritten disclosure output with no recoverable targeted information in content, image regions, earlier revisions or ancillary objects. Refuse unsupported content before releasing an artifact. | Confidentiality must survive extraction and forensic inspection. |
| R7: deployment | Reproducible .NET Linux x64 deployment, packaged fonts/native dependencies, no uncontrolled outbound calls, offline license operation, bounded workers and logs excluding document content. | Some customers prohibit sending documents or license telemetry externally. |
| R8: operations/contract | Vendor-supported version, security advisories and patch process, incident escalation, negotiated response times, permitted SaaS/OEM scope, legal entities and affiliates covered. | A ten-year retention service needs a support and maintenance plan. |

Offline means document processing and evidence-based validation can execute without internet access. Fresh public revocation data and trustworthy timestamps require a controlled connection, an internal service or previously obtained evidence. When evidence is missing/stale, return indeterminate and queue the action. An isolated worker cannot manufacture current revocation status or a trusted external timestamp.

Identity proofing, consent, jurisdictional signature policy, retention, key custody and qualification of trust services remain application and governance responsibilities. No library purchase establishes regulatory compliance by itself. The European Commission's [DSS documentation](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/DSS/webapp-demo/doc/dss-documentation.html) describes trust management, revocation handling and signature validation as distinct functions; its [FAQ](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/spaces/DIGITAL/pages/880312429/eSignature%2BFAQ) also identifies trusted-list and qualification handling.

## Typical selection process applied here

1. Obtain business, accessibility, security and records-team agreement on the artifact classes and gates. Decide which input types can enter a reviewed exception queue.
2. Request a written capability and licensing response against these gates from the shortlist, with precise package versions, dependencies and add-ons. Eliminate confirmed mandatory gaps before scoring price or developer convenience.
3. Review documentation and source where accessible. Compare equivalent licensed product packages, rather than a free core against a competitor's full suite.
4. Run a time-limited corpus pilot under identical limits. Record independent validation results, unsupported cases, implementation effort and operator recovery cost.
5. Review three-year costs and vendor contract. Approve only after a pilot exit review; hold signed originals and rollback artifacts independently of the selected library.

This report drafts the requirements and performs the documentary shortlist review within steps 1–3. Business-team sign-off, vendor responses, procurement negotiation and the comparative pilot have not occurred. Steps 4–5 describe the remaining acceptance and purchasing work.

## Evidence and shortlist

The evidence is asymmetric: ZingPDF's repository, test assertions and explicit limits were inspected directly. Competitors were evaluated from official documentation and licensing pages. Vendor documentation establishes a credible pilot path, not measured correctness or superiority on hostile documents. We did not penalize a competitor's undocumented edge cases as known failures or treat its marketing as proof of passing the gates.

| Candidate | Evidence that changes the decision | Desk decision |
|---|---|---|
| ZingPDF | Support contract limits high-level custom fonts to WinAnsi/Windows-1252; trusted timestamps, DSS/VRI and DocMDP validation are explicitly unsupported. No published PDF/A or PDF/UA conformance workflow was found. | Reject for this mandatory scope. Low price cannot compensate. |
| iText Core + pdfCalligraph + pdfSweep | .NET APIs document archival/accessibility generation, PAdES baseline profiles and validation; named add-ons address complex scripts and content redaction. Simultaneous conformance, sanitizer scope and hostile-document handling still need proof. | First pilot candidate with commercial license. |
| Aspose.PDF for .NET | Documents tagged-PDF creation, archive/accessibility standards and signature APIs; its signature facade exposes LTV state. Require exact B-LTA renewal, permission-validation and Linux/HSM demonstrations rather than inferring them from the word PAdES. | Credible challenger, particularly if existing-document conversion becomes a central requirement. |
| Apryse Server SDK | .NET signature verification exposes detailed results and unsupported features; PDF/A conversion/validation APIs exist. Obtain the exact server-side accessibility, shaping and production-package scope. | Credible challenger; package and feature-equivalence questions remain open. |

### Why iText leads this pilot

iText provides documented .NET paths closest to the mandatory signature lifecycle: `PdfPadesSigner` supports baseline profiles and prolongation, and its validation module is separately documented. This reduces the amount of custom cryptographic PDF machinery we would need to invent. [PAdES API](https://kb.itextpdf.com/itext/pades-signing-high-level-api), [validation API](https://kb.itextpdf.com/itext/signature-module-refactoring-built-in-signature-va).

Its .NET archival/accessibility examples and `PdfUADocument` describe conformance-oriented creation. These are well aligned with controlled templates; they do not promise that an arbitrary incoming PDF becomes accessible through conversion. [PDF/A and PDF/UA tutorial](https://kb.itextpdf.com/itext/chapter-7-creating-pdf-ua-and-pdf-a-documents-net), [.NET PDF/UA API](https://api.itextpdf.com/iText/dotnet/9.1.0/classi_text_1_1_pdfua_1_1_pdf_u_a_document.html).

pdfCalligraph explicitly covers Arabic, Hebrew and Devanagari shaping in .NET. pdfSweep supplies content-redaction functionality; ancillary-data sanitization remains a separate acceptance requirement. [pdfCalligraph](https://www.itextpdf.com/products/pdfcalligraph), [product packages](https://www.itextpdf.com/products).

The choice is conditional because these are product/API claims. PDF/A conversion of arbitrary uploads, high-fidelity preview rendering and automated remediation could change the winner. Apryse and iText also have common vendor-group exposure: iText's [copyright page](https://itextpdf.com/how-buy/legal/copyright-intellectual-property) identifies Apryse Group NV. Treating the two as independent supplier diversification would be misleading.

Aspose's [tagged-PDF documentation](https://docs.aspose.com/pdf/net/accessibility-tagged-pdf/), [signature documentation](https://docs.aspose.com/pdf/net/securing-and-signing/) and [signature facade API](https://reference.aspose.com/pdf/net/aspose.pdf.facades/pdffilesignature/) justify keeping it shortlisted, but detecting an LTV marker alone is not equivalent to validating the evidence. Apryse's [.NET verification guide](https://docs.apryse.com/core/guides/features/signature/verify-pdf/csharp) explicitly warns that verified signatures can leave some document content unsigned; its [PDF/A API](https://docs.apryse.com/core/guides/features/pdfa/apis) provides a conversion/validation path. Those distinctions are useful procurement questions rather than reasons to assume failure.

## ZingPDF findings from the repository

- `docs/project/SUPPORT.md` explicitly limits fonts and signature validation, and requires seekable streams. Signing currently requires unencrypted input/output. Some signing/rewrite paths stage full output in memory; instances are not thread-safe. These affect the multilingual, existing-document and resource-limit gates.
- `src/ZingPDF/PdfSignature.cs` implements byte-range/CMS validation and optional certificate-chain checks. `ValidateTimestamp` states trusted timestamp validation is not implemented, and strict `ValidatePermissions` reports DocMDP unsupported. An integrity-valid result must not be represented as a trusted long-term signature decision.
- `src/ZingPDF/PdfRedactionPlan.cs` performs structural removal and calls `RemoveHistoryAsync()` after applying marks. It is more than a visual overlay. It refuses inline images and shading; image handling is limited to supported encodings and 8-bit image XObjects. Exact glyph-to-operator mapping is required for text redaction. This explicit refusal behavior is valuable, but it is not a complete disclosure sanitizer.
- `tests/ZingPDF.Tests.Smoke/PdfTests.cs` contains signed-byte tamper, detached-signature verification and redaction/rewrite cases. `PdfRedactionPlan_TextAndRegionMarks_DoNotLeaveOriginalContentInRewrittenOutput` checks a generated fixture's removed content. These narrow tests establish useful intent and coverage; they are not a hostile PDF corpus, PAdES-LTA validator or proof of universal redaction safety. This evaluator inspected assertions but did not run a separate suite or competitor implementation.
- `docs/project/SUPPORT_POLICY.md` does not promise default contractual response times. `docs/legal/COMMERCIAL_TERMS.md` licenses seats within a legal entity and permits covered-version use after cancellation. A multinational product must obtain explicit affiliate and embedding terms, particularly if its exposed API could be considered a competing PDF service.

No amount of adapting the buyer's scoring model makes ZingPDF eligible while R2–R5 remain mandatory. Removing them would describe a different buyer.

The coordinating evaluator ran `dotnet test tests/ZingPDF.Tests.Smoke/ZingPDF.Tests.Smoke.csproj --configuration Release --logger "trx;LogFileName=technical-evaluation-smoke.trx"` on Windows: 104 passed, zero failed/skipped, approximately 16 seconds. Baseline: commit `5c421a9828dc3f88b891ed81e25976303720ea4a`, `VersionBase` 1.0.58, local `-dev` build, .NET SDK 10.0.301 targeting net8. This checks the existing smoke suite; it does not alter the mandatory-gap finding or prove Linux/conformance acceptance.

## Pilot acceptance and stopping rules

Use a frozen manifest with at least 200 permission-cleared external PDFs, 40 controlled multilingual/accessibility templates and 40 adversarial signature/redaction fixtures. Record input/output digests, package versions, platform image, fonts, configuration and independent validation artifacts. Cases may overlap; report all failures, not only successful examples.

1. **Controlled outputs:** every template must pass both selected profiles through a pinned local [veraPDF validator](https://docs.verapdf.org/validation/) after final signing. Accessibility reviewers must inspect reading order, table navigation, form labels and useful alternative text with assistive technology. Automated validation and visual inspection serve different purposes.
2. **Signatures:** cross-check B-T/B-LT/B-LTA and renewal with a locally deployed independent DSS implementation. Include tampering, revoked signers, missing/stale evidence, invalid TSA chains, appended revisions, permitted/forbidden certification changes and nested multiple signatures. No negative fixture may be labeled fully trusted. Changing an original's digest fails preservation immediately.
3. **Redaction:** seed canaries in text, images, nested/shared form XObjects, annotations, attachments, metadata, invisible/OCR text and prior revisions. Check extracted/decompressed objects, image pixels and forensic recovery; inspect retained material outside the marked region. Unsupported content must produce no releasable output. Do not replace this with a search for one literal string.
4. **Deployment:** run with egress denied and a read-only application filesystem; verify license behavior and dependency packaging. Use a controlled internal TSA/revocation service only for the signing tasks requiring it. Test worker timeouts, decompression limits, malformed/cyclic objects and cancellation without crashing the coordinator.
5. **Performance:** on a recorded 8-vCPU/16-GB Linux host, propose p95 under 5 seconds for a 20-page inspection/redaction job, excluding external signing-service latency, and peak worker memory below 2 GB for the agreed normal corpus. Exceptional bundles may use a documented slower lane. These are buyer targets, not measured library performance.

Stop immediately on confidentiality leakage, falsely trusted signatures, silently invalidated originals or uncontrolled external data transmission. Reject the pilot if controlled templates cannot meet both profiles, required languages fail, offline production licensing fails or the negotiated terms exclude the intended distribution. An unsupported adversarial input that is clearly rejected is an acceptable safe outcome; unsupported ordinary inputs must be counted and assessed against the buyer's exception budget, proposed at less than 1% of the representative corpus.

## Implications for ZingPDF

This segment is currently outside ZingPDF's defensible product scope. The immediate improvement is a clear buyer-facing boundary: basic detached signing and layered integrity/chain checks are not timestamp/DSS/DocMDP validation or PAdES archival-signature support. Current redaction is structural on supported content with refusals, and does not establish a universal hidden-data sanitation claim.

If pursuing this market, the highest-value work is to demonstrate a complete vertical slice: complex-script font embedding and extraction, semantically tagged controlled output validated for a named archival/accessibility profile, external signing with trusted timestamps and LT/LTA evidence, negative validation cases, and forensic redaction corpus results. These are separate engineering programs; shipping one does not imply the others. A negotiated support/security process and exact legal-entity/product-embedding contract are also required.

A narrower positioning can be commercially rational: serve PDF manipulation and controlled Western-text workflows, then let buyers use a specialized conformance/signature service where needed. For this buyer, a split stack only becomes eligible if interfaces, ownership of evidence and end-to-end gates are demonstrated. It is not a reason to select ZingPDF as the primary library today.
